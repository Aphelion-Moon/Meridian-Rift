dirt_drawing
/datum/unit_test/dirt_drawing
inherits /datum/unit_test/dirt_test

Dirt of every material drawn through the paths mess draws through. General dirt lands on each carrier that shows where its cause reaches, kept to those limbs. It stacks, paints bare bodies, lands on odd bodies and washes off. Sweat darkens the garments it lands on and is never left over. Parts drawn over the skin take the body's dirt. Taur bodies, taur suits, tails, wings, ears and horns carry their own, and the hair carries its own while it shows.

proc

()

Bodies the plain human template doesn't fit take a plunge into mud. They're a monkey, whose skin draws masks made for it, a lizard, and a body without its left arm, which draws nothing there. The lizard's head isn't a human's, so the face's stamps keep off it.

proc
check_packed_runs
()

A raster packed as text (/datum/dirt_pixels/proc/drawn()) and read back (dirt_pixels_from_drawn()) is the same raster. It has the same drawn pixels in row-major order, their roles and thicknesses and whether it covers, and it packs again as the same text. The rasters built by hand are: empty, one pixel at the canvas's end, a full row, seven thicknesses side by side, and the whole 64x32 canvas, with a run past 64 pixels and its last index among them. A jumpsuit holds three deposits and two shots of mess, with a third shot at the top level, which covers. Its merge of each direction from its packed texts (dirt_merge()) matches the merge of the rasters read from them. Its job's merges are the rasters merged that way, with the parts its mess reaches (the pecs guide a plain human chest takes, dirt_merge_with_parts()). A bare taur's body holds walking mud, slime on its back and two pools. It merges each direction on the 64x32 canvas, with no limbs. Its merge from its packed texts matches the merge of the rasters read from them.

proc
check_painted_grease

Machine work in grease on a bare body wears the hands' painted design, as machine work in oil does, in grease's own colours. Each limb bakes the pixels a bare body's oil bakes there. Each of those pixels takes grease's tint of the role that oil's pixel takes in oil's tints.

proc

proc
check_shape_memo
()

What a body's drawn parts are read from is kept between draws, and read again on every change to it. Each change below is drawn through update_body_parts() alone. A lizard's tail swapped for another accessory draws masks made for the new tail. A taur body holding a pool takes walking mud, and its masks take both. The rider's leg swapped for a digitigrade one has them made again. After each, the part draws the masks a fresh body in the same state draws. proc
check_stacking
()

General deposits stack. The same cause and material raise one deposit, keeping its variant. A fourth deposit on one carrier drops the one that landed longest ago. A deposit raised counts as landed when it was raised. Past DIRT_MAX_SPLATS, a person's splat that landed longest ago goes as a shot lands. The cap counts their skin and what they wear, an item in suit storage included. That item draws the splat no more, and its carrier, left holding nothing, is deleted. A splat raised counts there as landed when it was raised too.

proc
check_stain_over_sweat

A biped's jumpsuit takes soot, then glowing blood, then sweat. The blood landed after the soot, so its stain draws over the soot. On the emissive plane, the stain's glow draws over the soot's blocker. Sweat never draws over the stain, so once it lands the stain still draws over the soot and the sweat. Soot landing again after the blood draws its blocker over the glow.

An armband pinned on the jumpsuit where its sweat lies stays dry: the multiplier lies on none of the armband's pixels.

proc

A bare horse taur walking through mud takes it on its taur body, which draws it in a container over each of its layers' images. proc

proc
check_wag_frames
()

A lizard's short tail wags in nine frames that rewind. It's plunged into mud and doused in ash. Its plunge, clipped from a field drawn a band at a time, is the plunge drawn whole. Its douse drawn a band at a time is the douse drawn whole. With the douse taken off and the tail wagging, it draws its dirt once its frames are made. Smoke landing while it wags drops the frames, and none are drawn until they're made anew. Mud landing before they're made drops their job. Stopped while its frames for dried mud are made, it keeps their job until red clay lands, and asks for none while it's still.
