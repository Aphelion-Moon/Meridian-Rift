proc
add_placement
(/turf/tile, /list/art, ckey, who, pid, half, /list/evicted)

Places a piece's art on a turf, on top of its stack, and queues the map file's write.

The map's caps are met first, counting the account's own placements that its caps take: past them the lowest-ranked placement of an owner who isn't connected goes, and when nothing else can go the new paint is refused with the account's own placements left alone. Only a placement that lands takes the account's own lowest-ranked ones, as many as its caps need. Off the station levels, in shuttles and in the holodeck, the placement lasts this round only.

Arguments:

  • tile: The wall, floor or window tile.
  • art: The art, GRAFFITI_WIDTH wide. The registry keeps a copy.
  • ckey: The painter's ckey.
  • who: The painter's character name.
  • pid: The placement's id. The two halves of a wide piece share one; null makes a new one.
  • half: 1 or 2 for the left or right half of a wide piece, else 0.
  • evicted: When given, filled with the ids of the account's own placements its caps took, for the painter to be told how many went.

Returns:

  • text: The packed placement, which is in placements.
  • text: Why it wasn't placed, safe to show the player. It is never in placements.

proc
build_sheets
()

Packs every shown stack into sprite sheet files, chunk by chunk, and shows each decal from its sheet.

Decals are sorted by z level, 16-tile chunk and position, and each chunk's stacks are cut into sheets of up to GRAFFITI_SHEET_STATES, each stack in one sheet only. A sheet file is named by the hash of the stacks it holds and the alpha ceiling they are drawn at, so an unchanged chunk reuses the file of an earlier round, which clients have cached. Files no sheet uses are deleted, unless the map's graffiti isn't kept.

proc
load_file
(path)

Loads a map file in place of whatever the registry held, keeping only what is valid and within the caps.

Art that isn't valid one-tile art under its own hash, placements whose art is missing or whose fields have the wrong types, and the lowest-ranked placements and arts past the map caps are dropped and counted in one log line. A file that can't be read, or has a version GRAFFITI_MAP_READ_VERSIONS doesn't list, leaves the registry empty and unsaved, so the file stays for an admin. An older version's file is read as this version's, which its next save writes.

The current map's own file is named by graffiti_file_key() of its name. An admin-loaded custom map, whose name is whatever the admin typed, has no file: its store has no path, so nothing is read or written.

Arguments:

  • path: The file, or null for the current map's own.

proc

()

Loads the map's graffiti onto its tiles a share of each tick at a time (load_yield()), then starts noting who connects. Decals show their art once their chunk's sheet is drawn.

proc
load_yield
()

Lets the background load go on in this tick until it has taken load_share percent of it, or the tick is full, then waits for the next, so the load spreads evenly over the ticks and the server never feels it. Each wait comes between two pieces of the load, the largest a tile's stack merged or one icon drawn.

Returns:

  • null: Every owner is connected.

proc

(ckey, hash, size, replaced)

Makes room on the map for a placement of an art, a new one or a placement's new art: past the map's caps, counting the account's own placements that its caps take, the lowest-ranked placement of an owner who isn't connected goes, until the art fits or nothing else can go. Arguments:

  • null: Nothing else can go, so the paint is refused.

proc

(final)

Writes the map file: the art and placements that last past this round, and when their owners were last seen. A map that has never had lasting graffiti gets no file. Nothing is written before the map's graffiti has loaded (loaded), while the registry may hold only part of the file, nor after the round-end save.

The file is joined from text, as json_encode() would write the same tree: each placement is kept packed already, and each art record is encoded as it is joined, so the registry holds no second copy of its art between saves.
