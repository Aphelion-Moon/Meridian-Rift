Muscle size and definition

Module ID: FITNESS

Description:

Bodies drawn from the human and humanoid sheets come in three muscle sizes, each with four steps of muscle definition. A body has one size and one definition, for its chest, arms and legs alike.

Not offered yet. While MUSCLE_OPTIONS_OFFERED is FALSE, character setup hides the muscle inputs: the muscle preferences aren't accessible, so every character is drawn Normal with no definition.

Character setup. For a species whose own limbs come in muscle sizes, Body type has three inputs under it: a "Muscles - automatic" box, ticked by default, and a slider each for "Muscle definition" and "Muscle size", locked while the box is ticked. Automatic muscles will follow the character's stats, dexterity their definition and strength their size. Neither stat exists yet, so automatic means Normal with no definition. The sliders keep their steps while the box is ticked.

Limbs. Each limb drawn from the human or humanoid sheet, and each digitigrade leg they turn into, has a family of subtypes (<limb>/fitness, <limb>/fitness/athletic and <limb>/fitness/muscular) that differ only in the art they draw:

  • The plain limb is Normal with no definition.
  • <limb>/fitness is Normal with a definition.
  • athletic and muscular draw their size's sheet, at any definition.

A digitigrade leg draws no definition, so its family has the sizes only. A leg's size carries over to the digitigrade leg it turns into. The plain limb takes no definition: drawing one needs the fitness subtypes' overrides, and keeping them off the plain limb keeps its redraw as it was.

Sizes. The size sheets are icons/human_parts_athletic.dmi, human_parts_muscular.dmi and their humanoid twins, under the plain sheets' state names; Normal is today's sheets, untouched. The humanoid sheets are the human ones through the humanoid palette, except a few humanoid edge pixels and the hands, which are the humanoid sheet's own. A size limb draws its size's sheet wherever that sheet holds its state, and its own sheet otherwise (fitness_sheet()). Skin tones off move a human's limbs to the humanoid size sheets. Digitigrade legs keep their plain sheet and draw their size's state from the size sheets:

  • A human's digitigrade legs are the mammal ones: mammal_r_leg_digi and mammal_l_leg_digi, in the human size sheets.
  • A humanoid's are humanoid_r_leg_digi and humanoid_l_leg_digi, in the humanoid size sheets.

The Muscular sheets carry a rounder side-view biceps (one more pixel on the arm's front edge, r_arm E and l_arm W) and the male chest's glute bump (3 pixels behind the hand, chest_m E and W). In both size sheets, the west view's near leg mirrors the east view's.

Definition. The overlays are icons/human_definition.dmi and icons/humanoid_definition.dmi, one state for each state a limb draws, size and step: <drawn state>_<size>_<step>, like human_chest_f_muscular_2 or humanoid_r_leg_normal_1. An overlay holds only the pixels its step changes on its size's sprite, in the sheet's own greyscale, all inside the limb's outline: definition never changes an outline. The chest's overlays carry its abs, back and glutes.

A fitness limb with a definition draws its overlay (draw_muscle_definition()) as a copy of its own image, so it is coloured, offset for height and textured as the limb is. The overlay sits right above the limb, both halves of a leg, and so under its markings, custom markings, tattoos and everything else drawn on it. On a husk it's toned as the limb is (husk_toned_sheet()). No icon is made for it: the leg halves come from the masked leg cache, and a husk's tone is made once per sheet. Nothing is drawn where the overlay sheet has no state for what the limb draws: on hands, digitigrade legs, and a limb drawn from another sheet, like another species' look. Nor is anything drawn on a limb that draws nothing itself, like a taur's hidden legs.

Where size and definition live. The preferences write them to dna.features (FEATURE_MUSCLE_SIZE and FEATURE_MUSCLE_DEFINITION), before species, and swap the limbs (update_fitness_limbs()). A body's species points its own limb types at its size and definition when it is gained, so regrown limbs, and the limbs a change to another species builds, come out at the size and definition in the DNA. A change that keeps the species, such as a changeling taking another human's form, leaves the limbs the body has until they regrow.

A limb's size is its type, and its definition its own (muscle_definition): a new fitness limb takes the definition in its first owner's DNA. A limb keeps both when it is cut off and put back on, or put on someone else.

Clothing. Read a limb's size from muscle_size on /obj/item/bodypart, a MUSCLE_SIZE_* define, per limb: body.get_bodypart(BODY_ZONE_L_LEG)?.muscle_size. One leg can be a cybernetic one at Normal while the other is Muscular. Definition changes no outline, so it never changes a fit.

Underclothes. Underwear, bras, undershirts and socks are redrawn over the limbs they cover, at runtime, each limb at its own size (code/garment_fitting.dm). make_appearance() draws a garment's fitted sprite (fitted_sprite()): its state fitted, then cut for a female body, so a Normal body with no pecs under it gets today's sprites untouched. A body's redraw (update_body()) refits its underclothes, and a limb changing, regrowing or being swapped for an augment redraws it. A taur body's legs and chest aren't fitted, but its arms are.

A digitigrade body's legs are fitted through their own templates only where the legs drawn are digitigrade art: a _d state (a style with has_custom_digi_sprite), or the legs the GAGS digitigrade mask draws (digi_icon_state), which are cut and masked first and then fitted. A plantigrade state on a digitigrade body, like the babydoll or a one-piece swimsuit, is fitted as the human legs it draws, as worn clothing is.

Each style has a fit_mode (code/garment_fit_styles.dm):

ModeStylesWhat it does
GARMENT_FIT_SOLIDevery style by defaultMoves the garment's edge out to the limb's new edge and fills the gap with the pixel inside it. Rows above an arm's old top are fitted only for a sleeve, so straps stay put.
GARMENT_FIT_PATTERNfishnets and the shibari sleevesDraws only the new pixels, each copied from 2 pixels further in, so the mesh keeps its rhythm and rope its gaps.
GARMENT_FIT_SHADED_SLEEVESplain long sleevesSolid, then the arms' muscle shading in each sleeve's own colours, and the moved edge a shade darker.
GARMENT_FIT_KNITthe ribbed turtleneck and turtleneck leotardSolid, then the ribs laid again along each sleeve, its inner shadow thinned and its cuff ribbed.

Shaded sleeves take the size's own arm shading, at no definition, whatever the arm's definition.

Shaded sleeves step down the garment's colours, brightest to darkest, through each sleeve's own colours alone (sleeve_colours()): those the garment has over that arm, and its other colours of the same family. A lit colour is of the family when it's a grey and the sleeve has a lit grey, or when its hue is within 20 degrees of every lit hue the sleeve has, which keeps the sleeve one hue as garment_sleeve_mode() judges it. A dark one, an outline's or a shadow's, is of the family when it's a grey and the sleeve has a grey, or its hue is within 20 degrees of any the sleeve has. A garment of one material shades as it did with its whole ramp. A uniform's sleeve no longer takes its skirt's, belt's or boots' colour, which the whole ramp gave the sailor dress's and the cowboy uniform's moved edges.

Each step also keeps the stepped pixel's own family (garment_colours_kin()), so a sleeve of a hue and a grey doesn't step one into the other: DutchUniform's grey edge took its red, and janimaid's grey frill its navy. A step may go:

  • into a charcoal or a black, a dark near-grey, which shades and outlines every colour;
  • from a dark colour, an outline's or a shadow's, to another;
  • from a near-grey to another, a near-grey's channels spreading under 24% of its brightest, so a greyscale garment's own faintly blue shading stays one ramp;
  • or into a hue within 20 degrees of the pixel's hue, or of a charcoal's cast, its hue however slight: a grey never steps into a hue, while a blue-cast charcoal may step into navy, as the security uniforms' edges do.

A step that doesn't fit takes the next darker one that does, and a pixel with none is left as it is.

Sleeves. Whether a garment has a sleeve over an arm is judged once, from its front view, where the arms show whole: it does when it covers at least half of the upper third of the arm's baseline pixels there. A short sleeve covers the upper arm and counts; a strap covers a pixel or two of it and doesn't. Every view's fit uses that answer (garment_front_sleeves()), so a fit does the front views last, while they're still the garment's own (GARMENT_FIT_PIECE_VIEW()). A leg is judged the same way in each view, from the upper third of its own baseline pixels there. Under a sleeve:

  • The rows an arm grew into above its old top are fitted.
  • A solid row is also fitted where the garment covers its old edge alone, a hem a pixel thick against the arm's edge. The gap takes the edge pixel when the pixel inside it is bare, so the fit never opens a hole.
  • Side views, unless the garment is a pattern: the bigger arm's pixels the sleeve leaves bare, down to where today's arm begins, take the nearest sleeve colour in their row (close_hem()), so a short sleeve's edge doesn't stop partway across a bigger arm.

Polos. A polo's side views leave the near forearm bare below the short sleeve, edged at the back by a 1 pixel strip. The hem rule above takes that strip for a sleeve's one-pixel hem and moves it out across a bigger forearm, which then looked thin. The polos opt in to one more rule after it (bares_near_forearm(), on the four tg polos and Nova's): in each side-view row where the polo as drawn leaves some of today's near arm bare, the fitted polo is cleared over the whole near arm of its size (bare_near_forearm()), so the forearm shows at its own width. Rows its sleeve covers are fitted as before. Other shirts, and the polo-styled CentCom intern jumpsuit, keep their fit: the jumpsuit leaves the forearm bare with no strip, and its one pixel over a Muscular forearm is its own torso, as on the plain shirts.

Glutes. The Muscular male chest's glute bump, 3 pixels behind the hand in the side views, is covered by garments that reach the hips' back edge there: its bands ("chest_m" in the templates) are fitted after the legs, as a pec's side bulge is, so a garment covering the old edge and the pixel inside it moves out over the bump. Most bottoms, uniforms and suits are already drawn wide enough at the hips and stay as they are; what changes is the garments that stop at today's outline there, like the turtlenecks, the leotard and the mesh. The female chest and the Athletic one have no bump. A body's fit has the chest only while its chest is a fitness chest drawn male (limb_gender), so a fitness chest refits its body's garments each time it's set up for the body, which sets its male or female art.

What fitting costs. A body keeps its fit (garment_fit on /mob/living/carbon/human: each fitness arm's and leg's size, and its chest's where it has glutes to fit), worked out again only when its limbs or body shape change (synchronize_bodyshapes()), and null on a Normal body. A draw reads it, so a garment on a Normal body costs what it did before fitting existed, give or take one look. Fits are shared datums (get_garment_fit()), each with its digitigrade twin, its twins over each pec shape and, over pecs, its twins flexing from each flex sheet (flexing()), and a fitted sprite is cached once a round by its fit and what draws it, in nested lists rather than by a text key, and handed to the appearance as the cached resource. A fitted garment drawn again costs about what its Normal twin does: measured in a debug build, within 2 µs of it per draw, and 7 µs more for a top over pecs, which reads the pecs first.

A garment's first fit to a new fit is made by SSgarment_fitting (code/garment_fit_queue.dm): the garment is drawn as it is, the fit queued, and the slot drawn again once it's made, a view of one direction's frame at a time in the tick time other subsystems leave over, so no draw makes one. A fit asked for again while it waits only notes another body to draw again. A dummy, which is drawn for snapshots such as record photos and character previews, has its fits made as it's drawn, and so fills the cache for the bodies drawn after it. GARMENT_FITS_DEFERRED turns the queue off. finish_all() makes every queued fit at once, for tests and anything else that must see a body fitted now.

fitting/garment_metadata.json holds, for the static worn and underclothes sheets, which states pick shaded sleeves and each fitted state's views' colours, so a fit of one of their states reads only the pixels it moves; a generated icon, a female cut or a body shape mask is read from its pixels. Each sheet is checked against its hash the first time it's used each round, and one changed since the file was written is read from its pixels too. Regenerate the file whenever one of those sheets changes.

Worn clothing. A uniform, suit, shoes and gloves worn by a human are fitted the same way, as they're drawn (code/worn_fitting.dm). build_worn_icon() fits the icon it has built, after the female cut and every body shape mask that redraws the legs, and before the item's worn overlays, so its emissive blocker is drawn from the fitted sprite. Each direction and animation frame is fitted, and an animated state keeps its frame delays, read from its sheet; one whose sheet can't be read, like a generated one, isn't fitted. Glows the item draws from separate -emissive states of its own sheet get the same fit (fit_worn_glows()). Blood, dirt and damage overlays, and uniform accessories, aren't fitted.

  • Only an item in one of those four slots is fitted, and only a 32x32 state with four directions: never one held, on a mannequin or inside another item, nor a taur's 64x32 sheets. A taur body's arms are fitted, its legs and chest aren't.
  • A digitigrade body's legs are fitted through their digitigrade templates only where the legs drawn are digitigrade art: a *_digi sheet, or the digitigrade legs the GAGS mask draws over a plantigrade sprite (CLOTHING_DIGITIGRADE_MASK). A plantigrade sprite on a digitigrade body is fitted as the human legs it draws.
  • An item's mode is its own fit_mode if it sets one (on /obj/item/clothing). Otherwise shoes and gloves are fitted solid, and uniforms and suits by their sleeves in the front view of the icon drawn, GAGS colours included (garment_sleeve_mode()): shaded sleeves where they're one plain colour, solid where they're busy, dark or missing. A glow only shows its shape, so it's fitted solid, or as a pattern for a pattern garment.
  • Each fit is cached once a round by the fit and the sheet, state, item type, female cut, body shape and colours, never by wearer, and made as "What fitting costs" says. An animated state is fitted frame by frame.
  • A fitness arm, leg or chest above the Normal size put on or taken off redraws all four slots (refit_worn_clothing()): otherwise a leg redraws only the uniform and suit, and an arm only the gloves. A size from the preferences redraws them when its swap ends its render batch.

Pecs under underclothes. Pecs are part of the body's shape (shapes_body() on /obj/item/organ/genital): underclothes covering them draw over them, where any other genital is hidden. Pecs drawn so use their nippleless twin. A uniform or suit still hides them, and examine and interactions still read them as covered. A butt is hidden by underclothes, as before.

A top worn over the chest (an undershirt, a bra, or underwear that hides breasts) is fitted to pecs drawn under it, after its limbs, by the pecs' shape and whether they have nipples (pecs_under_underclothes(), garment_fit_shape on the pec shapes):

  • Front and back views: where a pec reaches past the chest's own side, the top's chest panel moves out over it, at most 2 pixels, its edge's own detail and all. Mesh copies its pattern, and a knit its ribs.
  • Side views: the pec's bulge past the body's front is fitted as a limb is.
  • Front view: the pecs' lower curve is shaded one step down, a "smile", on solid and shaded-sleeve tops. Knit and mesh tops aren't shaded; the GARMENT_FIT_PEC_SMILE_* defines pick the modes.

Tank tops hang straight over the pecs instead (hangs_straight_over_pecs(), which the styles drawing the tank_* states override): their front and back panels stay as drawn, and the pecs peek out past them at the armholes. The front view still shades the smile, and the side views still fit the bulge.

Fitting a top to pecs needs its front and back views' colours: from the sheet metadata for a static sheet's state, so it costs little more than the limbs' fit, and otherwise read from its pixels, about 2.3 ms more for a solid or mesh top.

Flexing pecs. While pecs flex (start_flex(), the *pbounce1 to *pbounce4 emotes), a top fitted to them moves with them (/datum/garment_fit_job/underclothes/flex): its limbs are fitted once a view, and the pecs on each frame of the flex from that frame's pec template (fitting/pec_flex_templates.json), tanks hanging straight as they do still. The frames are drawn over a state of the flex's own sheet, so the top keeps the pecs' delays and loops. A flex starting or stopping redraws the underclothes with the chest (set_animation_icon()), so the top starts in step with the pecs and stops with them, back to its still fit. A jiggle isn't fitted, and a top whose state is animated itself keeps its still fit.

Each flex's fit is made once a round by SSgarment_fitting, ahead of any flex: the first time a top is drawn over pecs, its fits over those pecs flexing from each of the four flex sheets are queued behind its still fit (fitted_sprite()), so the top moves from the pecs' first flex on. Nobody waits for them and nobody is drawn again for them. A flex that comes before its fit is made draws the top still all through, since a top that started moving partway would move out of step with the pecs, and asks for the fit itself, as it did before the warm-up. A dummy queues none, and has each made as the flex that needs it is drawn; so does every body with GARMENT_FITS_DEFERRED off. Measured in a debug build, a flex's fit takes 5-20 ms to make, a view of a frame at a time; a top drawn while flexing costs about 4 µs more than drawn still, and a still top over pecs about 0.2 µs more than before tops followed the flex, before the warm-up added its four cache lookups to each draw of a top over pecs.

The female cut over pecs. A female body with pecs, of any shape and whether they show or not, isn't shaped at the chest (female_cut_over_pecs(), code/female_cut.dm): a full cut on its uniform or underclothes keeps only its crotch (FEMALE_UNIFORM_LOWER_ONLY, through masking_helpers.dmi's full cut less the pixels of its top-only one, female_lower_cut_mask()), and a top-only cut, the one digitigrade legs, taurs and Ceruleans also get, isn't made at all. A male body and a body without pecs are cut as before. Pecs put in or taken out redraw a female body's uniform and underclothes.

Tops cut for breasts. Some tops have a version for a chest without breasts, which they draw on any chest without them, pecs or none, except a female body's without pecs; over breasts they're drawn as they are (chest_cut_state()):

  • The fishnet tops in both slots ("Fishnet - Sleeved", "Fishnet - Sleeveless" and their greyscale twins) have a bra drawn into them. Their _flat states are pure mesh with the same scoop neck. "Fishnet - Crew Sleeved" and "Fishnet - Crew Sleeveless", and their greyscale twins, are crew-neck mesh tees in the undershirt slot.
  • Pasties sit lower on a chest without breasts, over its nipples: _m on a bare chest, and _m1, _m2 and _m3 over pecs, bigger pecs and the biggest pecs, by their garment_fit_shape, with nipples or without.

Fitting templates. fitting/fit_templates.json holds, for each limb, muscle size and view, the rows its outline grew in, its baseline pixels and its fitted pixels' skin tones; the limbs are the arms, the legs, and the digitigrade legs, measured from the mammal sheet's, plus chest_m, the male chest's glute bands at the sizes that have them. A leg in a side view grows toward both its front and its back, and its rows never take the cap rule, which keeps straps on an arm's shoulder. fitting/pec_templates.json holds each pec shape's outline, pixels, side bulge and smile, and the chest's own outline, and fitting/pec_flex_templates.json the same for each frame of each pec flex sheet, with the sheet's frame delays; a frame's smile is the still smile, lifted in each column as far as the pec's crease lifts on that frame. Regenerate them whenever the size or pec art changes. Definition needs no templates. The files are compiled into the resource file and read as the server starts, with the sheet metadata (SSgarment_fitting).

Making the art. Edit the six sheets themselves, keeping the overlays inside their size's outline, and regenerate the templates after any outline change.

Height. tg makes a human taller or shorter with displacement maps that draw one torso row twice, or drop one or two. tg's maps do that at row 16 from the top, which is a pec's crease. A flex then drew its crease two pixels thick and jumping on a Taller or Tallest body, and squashed the pecs on a Shortest or dwarf one. Every human torso uses icons/height_cuts.dmi instead:

  • Taller and Tallest draw row 18 twice.
  • Shortest drops row 18.
  • A dwarf drops rows 18 and 19.

These rows are below every pec shape (rows 9 to 17, in every frame of every flex). The legs and arms keep tg's maps, and monkeys keep tg's torso maps. Medium, Short and Tall bodies don't move a torso row, and draw as before.
