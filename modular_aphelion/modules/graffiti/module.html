/tg/ Station 13 - Modules - Types

modular_aphelion/modules/graffiti/module.md

Graffiti

Module ID: GRAFFITI

Description:

Players paint their own layered pixel-art graffiti onto the station's walls, floors and full-tile windows with a spray can, keep their pieces in a library on their account, and the art stays on its map from round to round. A piece is painted live on the wall in the graffiti editor (freehand), or prepared in the editor and sprayed on in stages from the can. A tile stacks up to five pieces. Admins find, view and delete graffiti in the Graffiti Manager. Hung posters take graffiti too, over their own pixels only, for the round.

Pieces and art

  • Pieces. A library piece has a name, a description, a width of 32 or 64 pixels (one or two tiles), one palette of up to 63 colours (GRAFFITI_MAX_COLORS, alpha allowed) and one to four layers, each named and glowing or not. Layer grids use the custom sprites codec. graffiti_validate_piece() checks a piece strictly, as Import reads it, and graffiti_encode_piece() writes it out ("format": "aphelion-graffiti", version 1); an exported or imported piece is at most 32 KiB. graffiti_clean_text() decodes entities before it trims and escapes names and descriptions, so cleaning twice changes nothing. Names and descriptions live in the library only: the Custom Graffiti tab edits them, and placed graffiti carries neither, though another map's file keeps those its placements were saved with until that map next loads, so examine and the Graffiti Manager show neither.
  • One tile or two. A two-tile piece that paints nothing on its right tile is one tile wide: the editor saves it so though its canvas stays wide, graffiti_validate_piece() narrows it (graffiti_trim_wide()), so imports and library loads do, and the can paints it so. A library entry whose stored thumb has another width than its piece, as a load that narrowed the piece leaves, is flattened again.
  • Alpha. Placed graffiti is drawn at most GRAFFITI_ALPHA_CEILING (204, 80%) opaque, so the surface shows through it, and translucent paint is never under GRAFFITI_ALPHA_FLOOR (51, 20%), so it still shows. graffiti_color() stores a colour whose alpha reaches the ceiling as opaque #rrggbb, raises an alpha under the floor to it and refuses 0, so a stored colour's real alpha is the ceiling at seven characters and its own at nine, and a piece's palette keeps an alpha only for paint chosen below the ceiling. The windows draw each stored alpha as its share of the ceiling (shownColor(), owner's ruling 2026-10-19): the editor's canvas, its preview and the thumbnails show paint at the ceiling, stored opaque, fully opaque and 99% of it at 99%, as the alpha bar reads. Placed graffiti, its icons, sheets and glow masks, draws them at their real alpha and never above the ceiling, which art stored before the ceiling may pass at nine characters (graffiti_drawn_colors()).
  • Art. Placed graffiti keeps no layers. graffiti_flatten() merges them into one art: its width (w), its palette packed into one string of #rrggbbaa entries (pal, opaque as ff), one flat grid (grid) and one glow mask (glow), each grid run-length encoded by graffiti_encode_flat(), a run taking a length character and its palette index in one character, or two once a merge passes 63 colours (up to GRAFFITI_FLAT_MAX_COLORS, 4,095). The topmost painted pixel decides each pixel's colour and glow: opaque paint replaces what is beneath, and translucent paint blends over it in the real alphas by the standard over operator (graffiti_blend()), a blend whose alpha reaches the ceiling stored opaque. graffiti_art_hash() is the md5 of the four fields, so identical art shares one hash, one record and one icon. A 64-wide piece splits into one art per tile (graffiti_split_wide()), each half keeping only its own colours, so a half hashes like an identical one-tile art. graffiti_flatten_records() flattens layers that are already decoded, a paint job's or the live editor's frames, without encoding them first. The graffiti_flatten_bytes test pins one piece's flattened bytes and hash (dfb8008c48fd821254c6be4f72e292bc): a change to them would make every stored art record and sheet look new. Blending in real alpha changed them on 2026-10-19, before the branch was merged; sheet files are named for the ceiling too, so every map's sheets were drawn again once.

The spray can

  • Every spray can that paints has Stencils and Custom Graffiti tabs in its Crayon window, except the hellcan, lubecan and mimecan (graffiti_enabled()). Custom Graffiti shows the Unfinished slot (Resume, Keep, Discard), then the saved pieces, searchable and sortable, each with Paint, Edit, Export and Delete, and New, Import and Freehand. The library travels as static data only while the Custom Graffiti tab is open, as 50 pieces' thumbnails take 40 KB as typical art and up to 1.5 MB as adversarial art: opening the tab sends it, and it is sent again when its revision changes; a library purged and loaded again always takes a newer revision, so an open window never keeps a stale one. Its rows compare what they show (sameRow()), so a library sent again redraws only the rows that changed.
  • Renaming. Clicking a saved piece's name opens its name and description for editing in its row; Enter or Save sends them (graffitiRename), Esc or Cancel doesn't, and text the library holds already, once trimmed and with an empty name read as "Untitled", sends nothing. rename_piece() cleans them, makes an empty name "Untitled", writes through as a save does and is paced as one. The row shows the text sent until the library's text for the piece changes; a rename the library hasn't taken within 2 s, as one the write pacing refused, goes back in the form, open again at once unless another piece's is, else the next time this one's is. The Unfinished slot's name isn't editable.
  • Arming. Paint, Freehand and Resume arm the can (graffiti_armed: a piece id, "freehand" or "unfinished"), and its examine tells its holder what it is set to paint; anyone else reads only that it is set to paint a piece of graffiti, since the piece is from the holder's library. The next click on an adjacent wall, floor or full-tile window fastened down, on graffiti on one, or on a poster (see Posters), starts a paint job of the piece, opens a freehand session there, or resumes the Unfinished piece; a click on such a window stands for its tile, and an unarmed can still tints it. While the Unfinished piece's paint still shows on this map (graffiti_resume_tile(), found by its placement id), the armed can resumes it only there, a click on either tile of a two-tile piece standing for its left one: a click on any other surface is refused with "wrong wall!" ("wrong floor!", "wrong window!", "wrong poster!"), keeps the can armed and starts no cooldown. Each attempt waits out GRAFFITI_START_COOLDOWN (3 s) from the last, refused or not, and is refused before any costly work: by an empty can, a paint job already running, or a two-tile piece without a matching tile to its right (the can stays armed); a two-tile piece that paints nothing on its right tile paints one tile (graffiti_trim_wide()). A freehand session that starts uses the arming up. A paint job keeps it, so the next surface clicked gets the piece too, until a finished Unfinished piece disarms the can. A click on anything else disarms the can ("graffiti disarmed") and sprays as usual, and so do opening the can's window afresh, picking a stencil, and deleting, keeping or discarding the armed piece.

The graffiti editor

  • /datum/custom_sprite_editor/graffiti is the custom sprite editor's shell on a piece: the same canvas, tools, history and stroke pacing, with a layer panel, an inline colour picker with an alpha bar and a one- or two-tile width. A player has one editor at a time (SSgraffiti.editors). Library writes, the editor's saves included, are paced per account by their cost (see the library's write pacing).
  • Names. The editor doesn't edit names: a new piece is "Untitled" with no description, and Save, Save As and the Unfinished piece a session leaves take the name and description the library holds for the piece (take_library_names()), so a rename in Custom Graffiti while the editor is open stays. The piece as last saved takes them too, so a rename alone is no unsaved change: Save writes nothing and the session leaves no Unfinished piece.
  • Studio sessions (New, Edit) edit a piece for the library: Save writes it over its own id, Save As as a new piece. Keep saves the Unfinished piece directly to Custom Graffiti without opening the editor.
  • Updates. The window keeps every layer's view it was sent, so an update carries only the views that changed since the last one (sent_views) and null for the rest, each view with the serial of its codes (viewSerials), which changes whenever they do. A window that finds a view missing, as after a reload, or a null under a serial it doesn't have, as when two updates come before it draws, asks for them all again (canvasResync, taken even while strokes wait, when the drain's update brings them). After a stroke on a two-tile four-layer piece an update is 5.5 KB rather than 11.2 KB.
  • Freehand controls. Clear, eraser, selection, layer removal and undo/redo are unavailable during surface sessions. Paint stays once drawn; studio sessions retain their editing controls. A full surface stack refuses a new piece with "too much graffiti here!"; resuming a piece already in the stack still works.
  • Surface sessions (Freehand) paint live on a wall, a floor or a window's glass, and one on a window names the window in its title rather than the floor beneath it. The piece goes on top of the tile's stack once it has paint and is recomposited there as costly work under SScustom_sprite_work's overrun budget, at most every GRAFFITI_COMPOSITE_SPACING (1.5 s), or every GRAFFITI_COMPOSITE_SLOW_SPACING (10 s) once the player's GRAFFITI_COMPOSITE_BUDGET (600) composites for the round are spent, paint job steps included. Every painter's composites together, paint job steps included, take at most GRAFFITI_COMPOSITE_SHARE (100) ms a second of game time, and those waiting for room take it in the order they first asked: SSgraffiti's composite_turn() keeps them in composite_line, SScustom_sprite_work asks for each waiting painter every tick it gets through its queue, and one that hasn't asked for GRAFFITI_COMPOSITE_LINE_GRACE (1 s), as one no longer due doesn't, gives up its place. A session's last composite and a job's finish never wait but count (spend_composite()). So with many painters each one's composites come further apart, in turn, instead of time dilating. The tile's older graffiti shows under the canvas. A session ends when its window closes, after GRAFFITI_SESSION_LIMIT (15 minutes), or when the painter no longer holds the uncapped can, walks away or can't act, or the surface or the paint on it is gone. Ending charges the can once, GRAFFITI_COST (5) charges, half for taggers, when paint is left on the surface; a resumed draft left unchanged costs nothing. Paint left on the surface also marks each of its tiles as the painter's work, as a finished paint job does: their hidden print, and the art element once. It also gives the painter the Tagging Up Turf achievement (custom_graffiti_painted). The editor's Stencils and Custom Graffiti tabs end the session the same way and reopen the can's window on that tab. A surface session can turn two tiles wide only before its first placement.
  • Unfinished. A piece that changed since it was opened or last saved goes in the library's one Unfinished slot when its session ends. A freehand draft resumes only on its own tile, while every tile it painted still shows its paint (another surface is refused, as under Arming); a two-tile draft with nothing on its right tile leaves nothing there, and is kept and resumed one tile wide. A session ended by a pane loosened under it leaves none of its paint, on either tile of a two-tile piece, and its draft is kept as a plain draft, with no tile to resume on. Freehand drafts and stopped paint jobs share the slot.
  • Paste is refused on surfaces. A surface piece is painted by hand, so the workspace's prepare_selection_placement() refuses, with a notice, a selection placement that lands more pixels of any colour than it lifts or covers. Surface sessions disable the selection tool altogether, and the window hides Ctrl+V (noPaste). The staged reveal can't be enforced against a modified client, whose stroke masks may carry any pixels, so prepared art can be drawn in a few strokes, one per colour. That is bounded by the stroke budget and the composite pacing, and the charge is taken regardless (accepted).
  • Colours. The workspace counts the pixels of each colour as they change (color_counts), so checking a stroke against the 63-colour cap never reads the canvas. Paint whose alpha is 00 is refused, and a stroke, fill or paste lands its colours in graffiti_color()'s form, so one colour never takes two pixel values. The palette panel lists the colours in the order they entered the paint, then the picked ones the paint doesn't use. The inline picker paints with its colour as it changes and sends it (addColor) once an interaction ends, at a release, a hex commit or 300 ms after the last arrow key step, so a drag costs one act of the player's MINUTE_TOPIC_LIMIT, not one a move; a colour picked elsewhere meanwhile, a swatch or the eyedropper, drops the one not yet sent. Each addColor reply gives the brush the colour the server took (syncBackend()), so the picker lists the colours it sent until their replies come and refuses a reply a later pick overtook, putting the later pick back. Its alpha is a share of the ceiling: the alpha bar runs from the floor at its left end, 25%, to the ceiling at its right, 100%, sent as #rrggbb, so no interaction picks an alpha under the floor, and a fully see-through colour is never sent. On a piece whose paint uses all 63 colours (full) the picker mixes without changing the brush, and its title's tooltip says why. It sits under the colours, which scroll, so a piece gaining colours never moves it. The editor keeps the last GRAFFITI_PICKED_COLORS (16) picks, and one more drops the oldest even if it is painted, since painted colours stay listed through the paint.
  • Placing. A tile that refuses the paint, a new placement or more art for its placement, as a full map of connected painters' graffiti does, isn't asked again for GRAFFITI_PLACE_RETRY (10 s), and its refusal shows meanwhile; the session's last composite asks again.

Paint jobs

  • /datum/graffiti_paint_job sprays a saved piece on in stages: a do_after while each layer appears from the bottom up, left to right in strips GRAFFITI_REVEAL_STRIP (4) pixels wide. It takes GRAFFITI_REVEAL_BASE (8 s) plus GRAFFITI_REVEAL_PER_PIXEL (0.05 s) for each painted pixel, half that for taggers, at the painter's action speed as the do_after does. Steps come at most every GRAFFITI_REVEAL_MIN_STEP (1 s), a quick piece revealing several strips a step. A player runs one job at a time (SSgraffiti.paint_jobs).
  • The step timer only marks strips due. SScustom_sprite_work reveals them as costly work within its overrun budget, as it runs the editors' composites (both are /datum/custom_sprite_work_owners), so under load a step comes late and shows every strip due by then; steps are never dropped. The last strip shows only at finish(), when the do_after ends. Each step counts against the painter's GRAFFITI_COMPOSITE_BUDGET as a freehand composite does, though only freehand composites slow down once it is spent, and waits, as they do, while every painter's composites together leave no room (GRAFFITI_COMPOSITE_SHARE).
  • The can is charged once, never refused, as the piece's paint first shows (charge()), so a job stopped short of its last strip pays too and the job resuming it doesn't pay again. Finishing gives each decal /datum/element/art (GOOD_ART) once and the painter's hidden print, and the painter the Tagging Up Turf achievement. Stopping early keeps the paint revealed so far and puts the piece in the Unfinished slot with where to resume it: on the same map and tile, while that paint is still there, and a click on another surface is then refused (see Arming). Once that paint is gone it starts over wherever it is clicked. A job stopped by a pane loosened under it drops the paint it revealed for good, both halves of a two-tile piece, and its piece goes in the slot with nowhere to resume (graffiti_drop_loose_paint()).

Posters

  • A hung poster that isn't ripped takes graffiti over its own pixels. An armed can paints it as it paints a wall: Freehand opens the editor on the poster, and Paint or Resume sprays the piece in stages. The static-station rule applies where the poster hangs. One painter works on a poster at a time ("someone's painting it!"). A two-tile piece is refused ("too wide!"), and so is a piece that paints none of the poster's pixels ("won't show on the poster!"); the can stays armed for the first.
  • Its own pixels. graffiti_poster_look() reads a poster's icon state once, pixel by pixel, and keeps what it read for each icon and state (GRAFFITI_POSTER_LOOKS, 32; a wanted poster's runtime icon is read each time): the pixels it shows, as the editor's draw mask and bounds, and the poster itself as an art. The editor on a poster (/datum/custom_sprite_editor/graffiti/poster) puts them on its workspace, as the hair and markings editors keep paint to the body: the window shades the rest and its tools refuse it, and the workspace refuses it too (is_point_allowed()). The window draws the poster and its graffiti beneath the canvas, hides the width toggle (oneTile) and says the paint lasts the round. A paint job on a poster (/datum/graffiti_paint_job/poster) clips the piece's layers to the same pixels as it is set up (graffiti_clip_record()), so its time counts only the paint that shows.
  • On the poster, not the map. The poster keeps its graffiti itself (graffiti): every piece painted on it merged into one art, a new piece over the older ones as on a wall's stack, without a stack cap. None of it goes in SSgraffiti's registry, the map file, the sheets or the Graffiti Manager, so it lasts the round at most, as examine says. It draws from the art and glow icon caches the walls use, by art hash, so the same art on any number of posters and walls is drawn once. Its composites and job steps count against the painter's pace and every painter's composite share, as a wall's do.
  • Saving. Save writes the piece to the library as on a wall. A piece left unsaved goes in the Unfinished slot as a plain draft, with nowhere to resume, so Resume sprays it again from the start.
  • Taken down and hung again. Ripping the poster or taking down its wall deletes it with its graffiti. Taking it down with wirecutters rolls it up with the graffiti finished on it, out of sight ("rolled"), and hanging it again shows the graffiti once the poster is up (on_placed_poster()), clipped to the pixels the poster shows then. A piece still being painted stays behind, and its session or job ends. Cleaning washes poster graffiti off (CLEAN_TYPE_LIGHT_DECAL), which ends a painter's work there.

Tiles and stacks

  • /obj/effect/decal/cleanable/crayon/custom is the graffiti on one surface of a tile: a stack of placements, oldest at the bottom, shown as one merged composite. graffiti_surface_kind() says which surface a tile offers (GRAFFITI_KIND_*): a wall, the glass of a full-tile window fastened down on it (graffiti_window_of()), or a floor with ground; graffiti_surface_ok() is whether it offers one. A loose full-tile window still covers its floor and takes no graffiti until it is fastened again, and a directional window changes nothing. The decal keeps the kind it was made on (kind), and graffiti_tile_decal() finds the decal of the kind its tile offers now, so a floor's graffiti stays beneath a window fastened over it later, as a decal of its own, through any turf change that leaves the floor. A two-tile piece needs both tiles to take it and to be of one kind, walls, floors or windows (graffiti_wide_ok()); its halves are two placements sharing one placement id.
  • Windows. Graffiti on a window is drawn on the game plane at GRAFFITI_WINDOW_LAYER (3.25), over the glass and under closed shutters and blast doors, which share tiles with full-tile windows at 3.3, and follows its window: it goes for good when the window is deleted or leaves its tile, which is judged once the move is over, so a shuttle carries both, and it washes off with the window, which soap and sprays reach instead of its tile. Unfastening the window keeps finished graffiti, but a session or paint job painting the pane stops and its piece in progress goes for good, both halves of a two-tile one, as a cleaning takes it, with nothing to resume.
  • At GRAFFITI_STACK_CAP (5) the tile refuses new pieces, preserving its existing paint. While someone paints a tile, its decal keeps the merge of everything beneath the top piece and of the whole stack, so a live update merges only two arts. A stack shown from its sheet, changed by a removal, or left by its painters (graffiti_release_merges()) keeps neither (release_merges()): the first composite there merges the pieces beneath again, and the editor's backdrop merges them with base_merge() and stack_merge().
  • Examining it says "A piece of graffiti.", "It won't outlast the shift." of a round-only top piece, and "Older graffiti shows through underneath." while an older piece still shows. Whether one does is merged once, when first examined, and kept until the stack changes, as merging five pieces takes tens of milliseconds.
  • Glow. Paint on glowing layers lights on the emissive plane. A stack that glows everywhere gets an emissive of its icon, one with mixed paint an emissive blocker with an emissive of its glow mask just above it (the sheet's <stack key>_g state), and one that doesn't glow a blocker. The overlays take the decal's own layer, over the glass on a window; a floor's moves onto the emissive plane as emissive_appearance() moves topdown layers.
  • Cleaning. Soap and other cleaning remove the decal, and the registry drops its placements for good. A decal also goes when its turf changes and the surface it was made on is gone (NeverShouldHaveComeHere()), as when a wall turns into a floor or a floor into space: a floor's needs only its floor, so it lasts under a window fastened over it later, and a window's goes with its window. Cleanbots set to clean drawings erase graffiti too (accepted: it is cleaning, behind an explicit bot setting).
  • Decals loaded from the map file get no art element and no hidden prints: both belong to the round the paint went on.

The map registry (SSgraffiti)

  • Placements are kept as one packed JSON string each (json_encode() of the anchor, the art hash, ckey, who, at, pid, half and miss; 691 bytes, against 1,155 as a list, measured while placements still carried a name and a description), read with unpack(). They carry no name or description. placement_info indexes what placement operations read (ckey, art, at, id, the painter's character name, round-only) without unpacking, and ckey_placements and ckey_arts index each account's placements, in eviction order, and its art.
  • Timestamps (at, and seen for when an owner last connected) are ten-digit unix seconds kept as text (graffiti_unix_time()): BYOND numbers can't hold them (json_encode(1792800001) gives 1.7928e+09), and ten-digit text sorts in time order.
  • Caps and eviction. A map keeps GRAFFITI_MAP_PLACEMENT_CAP (4,000) placements of GRAFFITI_MAP_UNIQUE_CAP (2,000) unique arts of at most GRAFFITI_MAP_ART_BYTES (5 MiB) in all, and an account GRAFFITI_CKEY_PLACEMENT_CAP (400) placements of GRAFFITI_CKEY_UNIQUE_CAP (200) arts on each map. A new placement meets the map's caps first (make_room()), counting the account's own placements that its own caps take (own_evictions(), map_fits()): past them the lowest-ranked placement of an owner who isn't connected goes (lowest_unconnected()), and when every owner is connected the new paint is refused ("the walls here are caked in paint") and the account keeps all of its own. Only a placement that lands takes the account's oldest placements past its caps, and the painter is told, by the editor's notice or a paint job's balloon alert. New art for a placement, as a freehand session or a paint job reveals more of its piece, meets the same caps, the placement giving up its old art (art other placements share stays). It never evicts the placement itself, and when nothing can make room the placement keeps its old art: a paint job stops there, its piece going in the Unfinished slot, and a session's tile waits out GRAFFITI_PLACE_RETRY. rank() puts a connected owner first, then the owner seen most recently, then the newest placement, then file order; it also decides what a load drops past the placement and unique-art caps. So a full map can evict an offline player's oldest art.
  • Static station only. Graffiti goes only on the station as the map built it (graffiti_static_station()): a station level's turf in a station area, neither a shuttle's nor the holodeck's, and outside every map template loaded on the station this round, such as a random room, which the /datum/map_template/load() override records (SSgraffiti.loaded_templates). Other levels, bitrunning and reserved space, ruins and maploaded rooms take none: graffiti_surface_ok() and graffiti_wide_ok() refuse them, so the armed can says "can't paint here!" and stays armed. Condos will take graffiti through an anchor resolver of their own (below).
  • Anchors. graffiti_anchor_for() gives a static station turf a station anchor (its coordinates, its z level's place among the station levels and its area type), recording the turf's kind of surface as its surface. An anchor resolves only to a turf that is still the static station and offers the same kind of surface, so a window's graffiti counts a miss in a round its window isn't there, and a station anchor only in its old area. An anchor saved before windows took graffiti records wall, whether its surface is a wall, which check_anchor() reads as a wall or a floor, for a map file and for a stopped piece's resume data alike. Map templates took template anchors until 2026-10-21; a file's placement that still has one is dropped as the file loads. Resume data keeps the anchor of the piece's left tile with the kind of surface its paint went on (graffiti_resume_anchor()), and a piece that stops while a tile of it offers no surface, as under a loosened pane, keeps none. Other anchor kinds go through resolvers registered with graffiti_register_anchor_resolver().
  • Custom maps. An admin-loaded custom map (CUSTOM_MAP_PATH) keeps no graffiti past the round, and its paint is the only round-only graffiti: the placement keeps its turf's coordinates and is never saved. Its name is whatever the admin typed and may be another map's, so load_file() gives its store no path: no map file is read or written, and build_sheets() deletes no sheet file. graffiti_anchor_for() gives its turfs no anchor, so all of its paint is round-only and the editor and examine say so.
  • Dormant placements. A placement whose kind has no resolver this round waits without counting a miss. One whose anchor doesn't resolve otherwise counts a miss each round and is dropped once it has missed GRAFFITI_MISS_LIMIT (20) rounds in a row, as a station anchor does that the static station no longer covers. When an owner connects (on_client_connect()), their dormant placements whose anchors resolve now are shown. Everything within the caps shows (GRAFFITI_DISPLAY_BUDGET).
  • graffiti_registry_release() takes one unloading space's graffiti off its tiles and keeps it dormant in the file. With graffiti_register_anchor_resolver() it is the integration point for condos and districts; neither has a caller yet.

Saving and loading

  • Loading in the background. The map's graffiti loads once every subsystem has initialized and the game reaches the lobby (COMSIG_TICKER_ENTER_PREGAME), so it takes none of the server's startup: load_in_background() reads the file, places the stacks and draws the sheets a share of each tick at a time, at most GRAFFITI_LOAD_SHARE (20) percent of it (load_yield(), load_share), the largest piece in between being the file's one json_decode(), a tile's stack merged or one icon drawn. Decals show their art once their chunk's sheet is drawn. Until it has loaded (loaded) nothing is placed ("graffiti still loading!"), saved or deleted by an admin, and players who connect meanwhile are noted once it has. Unit tests load at once, with the whole tick (load_share 100), as they read the registry straight away.
  • The map file is written GRAFFITI_SAVE_DEBOUNCE (60 s) after a change, with every change made meanwhile, and last at round end (SSpersistence/collect_data(), after which nothing is written) and in Shutdown(), for a reboot that skips the round end. save_now() writes nothing before the map's graffiti has loaded, so a shutdown during the load can't write a half-loaded file. Round-only placements and art without paint aren't saved, seen keeps owners only, and a map that never had lasting graffiti gets no file.
  • The text is joined from the packed placements and from the art records, each encoded as the file is joined, so the registry keeps no second copy of its art between saves: the same text json_encode() would write. write_text() stages it (.new), makes the backup (.bak) hold the last verified revision, writes the primary, then copies it over the backup, and checks each file by its md5; only the md5 of the revision stays in memory. So once a save is written, graffiti cleaned before it is in neither the map file nor its backup (its sheet files go at the next load), and a write interrupted on the primary still recovers the last revision from the backup. Three backups are left holding an older revision until the file's next save: one written before saves refreshed their backups, one a crash leaves between writing the primary and refreshing the backup, and one kept for a primary deleted by hand. A map file edited by hand mid-round is kept as the backup until the next save and overwritten, with a log line. json_encode() writes non-ASCII as \u escapes, so map files are ASCII. A save that fails is logged, and the round's first failure is told to the admins (message_admins()), since the changes since the last save are lost unless a later save succeeds. A backup that can't be copied after the primary is written leaves the save standing, as the primary holds it, with a log line, and the next save copies the backup again, as the library's save() does.
  • Loading keeps only valid one-tile art under its own hash and well-formed placements. An art under its own hash that keeps its glow kind (k) is taken as the server wrote it, without decoding it; one without is decoded, checked and given its kind. It cleans their painters' names again, reads an old anchor's wall as its surface, leaves out fields it doesn't keep, such as the names and descriptions placements once carried, drops exact duplicates and whatever is past the caps, the art bytes included, by rank, and logs one line for everything dropped. Map files are version 2 (GRAFFITI_MAP_VERSION), and version 1, whose anchors may record wall and whose placements may carry names and descriptions, loads too (GRAFFITI_MAP_READ_VERSIONS): its next save writes it as version 2, while the Graffiti Manager's edits of another map's file keep its version. A file that can't be read, or has another version, leaves the map's graffiti empty and unsaved for the round, so the file waits for an admin. The stack cache. The file also keeps, for every saved stack of several pieces, its key and glow kind under its signature (its pieces' art hashes, stack_signature()), beside a fingerprint of what merging gives (graffiti_merge_fingerprint(): the alpha ceiling and the hash of one fixed merge). While the fingerprint matches, the load shows such a stack under its cached key without merging it (adopt_stack()), and merges it only should its sheet need drawing or someone paint the tile; a changed merge sets the whole cache aside. A warm load of 1,548 dense pieces in stacks of five took 1.5 s against 18.2 s merging, and in stacks of one 1.9 s against 9.2 s.
  • Sprite sheets. As the map loads, every shown stack's composite and glow mask is drawn into per-chunk sheet files: decals sorted by z level, 16-tile chunk and position, up to GRAFFITI_SHEET_STATES (32) composites a sheet, each sheet named by the md5 of GRAFFITI_ALPHA_CEILING and its stack keys (data/graffiti/sheets/<map key>/<hash>.dmi), so a sheet drawn at another ceiling is drawn again. Decals show them through fcopy_rsc(file()), which holds 0 bytes of server memory, and an unchanged chunk reuses its file from an earlier round, which clients have cached: the live check saw unchanged sheets never downloaded again. Files no sheet uses are deleted after the build. Paint changed mid-round shows from per-tile icons in the art and glow caches (SSgraffiti.art_icons and SSgraffiti.glow_icons, GRAFFITI_ICON_CACHE, 128 each, the oldest going first); an art no placement uses any more leaves both at once (release_art()), while the composite of a stack of several pieces, which no placement names, only ages out, and clearing the map empties them. Sheets are drawn from fresh icons (use_cache = FALSE): BYOND refuses every further Insert() into a sheet once about twenty icons inserted into it are still held elsewhere, as the caches hold theirs (graffiti_registry_sheet_chunk covers it).
  • The decode cache. During the load SSgraffiti.decoded keeps the decodes the load makes, of art without a kept glow kind, for the merges and sheets, within GRAFFITI_DECODE_BUDGET (8 MiB, about 400 dense one-tile arts); art past it is decoded again where needed. It is pruned to what the sheets draw once the stacks are placed and dropped once the sheets are drawn, or by a ten-minute timer should a runtime stop the load. The load's loops skip whatever changed while they waited for a tick.

The library

  • /datum/graffiti_library holds an account's GRAFFITI_LIBRARY_CAP (50) pieces and the Unfinished slot in one file of at most 2 MiB. Each entry keeps its flattened art as thumb, so the tab never flattens fifty pieces to open. Every change is written through and undone when the write fails, so the library holds what its file doesn't only while a change waits for the write pacing. Export downloads a piece as a file and Import validates an uploaded one, sharing the custom sprites transfer slot and cooldowns.
  • Empty libraries. A write that leaves the library with no pieces and no Unfinished piece deletes its file, backup and staged copy instead (delete_files()), as the admin purge does. The library stays loaded, next_id and all, and its next save writes the file again.
  • Unloading. A library unloads as its owner disconnects (graffiti_library_unload(), from /client/Destroy()), unless an editor session or paint job of the account's, a write the pacing holds back, or an import or export still holds it. /client/Destroy() runs before the owner's windows close, so an editor or paint job that ends, and a held write once made, unload it while its owner is away; one an import or export held stays until the owner's next disconnect, or the reboot at the round's end. graffiti_library() loads it again when it is next wanted, under a newer revision.
  • Write pacing. Each write rewrites and checks the whole file, 139 ms on average for 50 adversarial pieces, so it paces the account's next one by its cost (graffiti_library_paced()): a player's save, Save As, import, rename, delete, Keep or Discard waits at least a second after it, and GRAFFITI_LIBRARY_WRITE_PACE (20) times as long as it took (graffiti_library_write_due()), which holds one account's writes to a twentieth of the server's time. The changes the server makes are never refused: the Unfinished piece kept as a session or paint job ends, and a finished job emptying the slot, wait out the pacing and are written once by a timer with whatever else changed meanwhile. A player's write takes them along, and the round end and Shutdown() write whatever is still held (graffiti_library_flush()).
  • /datum/json_savefile/custom_sprites/graffiti is the custom sprites store for graffiti files: it loads every top-level entry through the store's load_entries() hook, is held to its size_limit on reading and writing, decodes its file as hostile input, and keeps only the md5 of the last verified revision in memory. Its save(), which the Graffiti Manager's edits of other maps' files use too, writes each revision it verified over the backup as well, as write_text() does: a deleted piece or a discarded Unfinished piece is in no file once the write that drops it is done, and a file changed outside the server is kept as the backup until the next save. A backup it can't write after the primary leaves the save standing, as the primary holds it, so the library keeps the change, and the next save writes the backup again.

The Graffiti Manager

  • The Graffiti Manager verb (R_ADMIN) opens /datum/graffiti_manager: the current map's placements, shown, dormant and round-only, GRAFFITI_ADMIN_PAGE (24) a page, sorted by date, ckey or area and searched by ckey or character name (who), each with a thumbnail and a larger view. Jump moves the admin onto the piece's own turf, which for a wall piece is inside the wall, as tg's Jump To Turf does. Deleting the selected pieces (a wide piece loses both halves), an account's graffiti on this map or everywhere (its library too), or this map's or every map's graffiti each asks first, re-checks R_ADMIN, and is logged and told to the admins with the count. Selected pieces and an account's leave the registry at once, and then each tile they leave is merged again once, yielding between tiles (remove_placements()), the pieces still stacked there keeping their order; editing the other maps' files yields between files. Deleting this map's graffiti, a piece, an account's or all of it, also ends what its painters have under way on it with nothing kept (drop_work()): a freehand session or paint job painting it stops without an Unfinished piece, and an Unfinished piece left to resume on it is discarded, an offline owner's too, whose library is loaded for it, yielding between them, and unloaded again. The bulk ckey target is the selection's single ckey, else the search text only when it is already a ckey. The window refreshes only after its own actions, since listing reads every placement.

The budget defines and where their values come from:

  • GRAFFITI_MAP_UNIQUE_CAP 2,000: 2,000 dense art records of 3,670 bytes take 7.3 MB.
  • GRAFFITI_MAP_ART_BYTES 5 MiB: the bytes of art a map keeps, each unique art's pal, grid and glow text counted (SSgraffiti.art_bytes, kept as art comes and goes), held like the unique-art cap: add_placement() evicts by rank, then refuses, and a load keeps the best-ranked placements whose art fits. About 1,500 dense one-tile arts or 400 translucent ones of 1,000 colours (12-14 KB a tile) fill it, while typical art meets GRAFFITI_MAP_UNIQUE_CAP first. The owner halved it from 10 MiB on 2026-10-20: 10 MiB of art and 4,000 placements wrote about 13.5 MB, so 5 MiB writes about 8 MB, and the arts held at the caps fall from at most 10.8 MB to about 5.4 MB (derived from the measured 10 MiB figures, not measured again). Art that grows as it is painted can pass the budget by what the pieces being painted add; the next new art evicts back under it.
  • GRAFFITI_MAP_PLACEMENT_CAP 4,000: a placement packs to about 271 characters, 1.1 million for 4,000, which take about 1.2 MB (measured on a full map), and their decals at 2,261 bytes take 9.0 MB at worst, one tile each, or 3.0 MB in stacks of three. At the map's caps the module holds, besides its placement indexes, which weren't measured, the arts (at most about 5.4 MB under GRAFFITI_MAP_ART_BYTES), the placements 1.2 MB, the decals as above and the two icon caches 1.5 MB, since sheet files keep every other icon at 0 bytes of server memory: about 17 MB with one tile each and 11 MB in stacks of three, from 22.5 and 16.5 MB with 10 MiB of art. Before the stroke and memory pass the registry also kept each art's save record (art_text, about the art's size again) and every stacked decal its merges (13.3 million characters for 1,334 three-high stacks of 6.8 MB of art): about 33 MB with one tile each and 42 MB in stacks of three. So the pass halved the stacked layout, 42 to 16.5 MB, but took one tile each only from 33 to 22.5 MB, short of half; what remained was the arts and decals the caps allow, which halving the art bytes took on. Each art is held once as its list and encoded at every save, about 50 ms more a save on a full map, rather than as its packed text decoded on demand, because a dense record takes 3,670 bytes as a list against about 3,500 as text, under 0.5 MB at the caps, while every use would decode the text again: json_decode() of about 3.5 KB, about 0.07 ms going by the 150 ms an 8 MB map file takes, before the 1.2 ms grid decode a merge needs, or a decode cache holding about 20 KB a dense art. A load adds up to GRAFFITI_DECODE_BUDGET (8 MiB) of decoded art while it runs.
  • GRAFFITI_DISPLAY_BUDGET: GRAFFITI_MAP_UNIQUE_CAP, so everything within the caps shows.
  • GRAFFITI_CKEY_UNIQUE_CAP 200 and GRAFFITI_CKEY_PLACEMENT_CAP 400: a tenth of the map's caps.
  • GRAFFITI_ICON_CACHE 128: 128 dense art icons of 4,801 bytes take 0.6 MB, and 128 glow masks of 6,930 bytes 0.9 MB.
  • GRAFFITI_SHEET_STATES 32: of the packings whose sheet files stay within 256 KiB, the one with the fewest bytes a state on worst-case art: 1,791 bytes at 32 (largest sheet 113 KB), 1,811 at 64 (225 KB), and 128 reach 448 KB.
  • GRAFFITI_DECODE_BUDGET 8 MiB: about 400 dense one-tile arts at about 20 KB decoded, which bounds what a load adds to the high-water mark. Keeping every decode was estimated at 40-140 MB on a full map.
  • GRAFFITI_SAVE_DEBOUNCE 1 minute: a full map of dense art takes about 440-470 ms to write, and the round-end save and Shutdown() still write the last changes.
  • GRAFFITI_PLACE_RETRY 10 s: set when a refused placement on a full map read every placement (577 ms each, 2.1 s for the first composite after a load); the per-ckey index has since brought a refusal down to 0.02 ms.
  • GRAFFITI_LIBRARY_WRITE_PACE 20: 50 adversarial pieces take 139 ms to write on average and 209 ms at most, 14-21% of the server's time at one write a second; twenty times a write's cost holds an account to 5%.
  • GRAFFITI_MAP_MAX_BYTES 16 MiB (a full map of dense art writes 7.6 MB), GRAFFITI_LIBRARY_MAX_BYTES 2 MiB (50 adversarial pieces write 1.74 MB) and GRAFFITI_PIECE_MAX_BYTES 32 KiB.
  • Pacing: GRAFFITI_COMPOSITE_SPACING 1.5 s, GRAFFITI_COMPOSITE_BUDGET 600 and GRAFFITI_COMPOSITE_SLOW_SPACING 10 s; GRAFFITI_START_COOLDOWN 3 s; GRAFFITI_SESSION_LIMIT 15 minutes. GRAFFITI_COMPOSITE_SHARE 100 ms a second, with a GRAFFITI_COMPOSITE_SHARE_BURST of 100 ms, of every painter's composites together: a typical one-tile composite takes 11-15 ms, so about ten painters keep the full spacing and more take turns. Set 2026-10-20 against the hundred-painter benchmark, which it took from +57.2% to +3.0% time dilation on one build (the share held open as the "before"), and twenty adversarial painters from +16.0% to +0.8%. GRAFFITI_COMPOSITE_LINE_GRACE 1 s: the composite line is first come first served, which without it gave twenty painters 3 to 11 composites a minute each and with it 8 or 9; a painter that stops asking for a second gives up its place, so one no longer due never holds the line.

The rest set the piece format (GRAFFITI_MAX_*, the widths and height, GRAFFITI_FLAT_MAX_COLORS, GRAFFITI_FORMAT*), stacks and costs (GRAFFITI_STACK_CAP, GRAFFITI_LIBRARY_CAP, GRAFFITI_COST), the reveal (GRAFFITI_REVEAL_*), anchors (GRAFFITI_ANCHOR_*, GRAFFITI_MISS_LIMIT), map file versions (GRAFFITI_MAP_VERSION, GRAFFITI_MAP_READ_VERSIONS), surfaces (GRAFFITI_KIND_*), the layer of graffiti on windows (GRAFFITI_WINDOW_LAYER), glow kinds (GRAFFITI_GLOW_*), paths (GRAFFITI_*_DIRECTORY), the placement index (GRAFFITI_INFO_*), GRAFFITI_WORK_COMPOSITE, GRAFFITI_SPRAY_LENGTH, the editor's picked colours (GRAFFITI_PICKED_COLORS) and GRAFFITI_ADMIN_PAGE. The alpha ceiling and floor (GRAFFITI_ALPHA_CEILING 204 and GRAFFITI_ALPHA_FLOOR 51, 80% and 20%) mean every piece of graffiti lets the wall show through, a piece keeps an alpha only for paint chosen below the ceiling, and no paint is too faint to see.
